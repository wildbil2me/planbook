Before anything else

Not on your real year

A pass is permanent history: every trip goes into passes and nothing in the app takes one out. Four ten-minute test passes on a real student leave four ten-minute trips on their record. Run this on the test install served from the laptop, with made-up students. It has its own storage and cannot see your real year.

Don't touch the screen while you wait

Any touch creates or wakes the audio, which is exactly what the tests are trying not to do. Once a pass is out, hands off until the step says otherwise. Put the iPad on a stand and set a timer on your phone, not the iPad (run 3 is the only exception).

The default stays off

You're switching the sound on for this one install with the header speaker. Nothing here changes what any other device does. Bringing the tone back for everyone is a decision to make after these results, not before.

What a pass sounds like

Two thresholds, one tint each, one tone each

When a pass reaches 5 minutes the card tints and the first tone plays. At 10 minutes it tints again and the second tone plays. The tint needs no audio at all, so a tint with no sound means the alert fired and the audio lost it. That's the failure this page is looking for. No tint at all is a different problem; write it down if you see it.

A tone should play only at a threshold, or when you switch into the app while a pass is already overdue. A tone at any other moment, like tapping Return or sending someone out, is the erratic behaviour from 16 August, and it's worth more than any pass or fail on this page.

The test install, sound on

On the laptop
  1. Start the app server: node tools/serve-https.mjs. It prints the LAN address. Or ask a session to start it for you.
  2. In a second terminal, start the probe server on other ports: node tools/serve-https.mjs --port 8444 --http-port 8081. Run 1 needs it, because on port 8443 the app's service worker opens the app instead of the probe page.
On the iPad
  1. If the iPad already has the Planbook install from https://<address>:8443 (August's test icon), use that one. If not, open http://<address>:8080/ in Safari and follow the setup page to install it. Don't use the icon you teach with.
  2. In the test install, make one class (for example English I (test)) and paste two or three made-up students: Test A, Test B, Test C.
  3. Force-quit the test install from the app switcher, then open it again. A reload isn't enough.
  4. Open About and check the build line reads planbook-shell-v147, with no amber line saying the screen is older than the stored copy.
  5. Turn off Silent Mode in Control Centre (the bell) and turn the media volume up.
  6. Tap the speaker in the header so it's no longer slashed. That switch is saved on this install only.

Control ~2 min

Can this iPad make the sound at all today?

The probe page plays the app's exact notes in different ways. It was the quickest way to tell a device problem from an app problem in August, and two of its probes are the ones that matter now.

  1. In Safari (not the installed app), open https://<address>:8444/tools/audio-probe.html.
  2. Tap probe 1. It should sound straight away.
  3. Tap probe 5, then take your hand away. It should sound about 8 seconds later with nobody touching the screen. This is the same way the app holds its audio.
  4. Tap probe 6 and take your hand away. Same wait, using an audio element instead. This is the fallback if the app's way turns out not to work.
Record

In August all three were audible. If probe 1 is silent now, check Silent Mode and the volume before anything else. If probe 5 is silent but 1 isn't, stop here and bring that back: it's new, and it changes the whole work order.

The failure ~11 min

An interruption that leaves the app on screen

This is the exact test that failed on 16 August: both tones silent while the card tinted on time. The interruption has to happen without Planbook leaving the screen. Switching apps or locking the iPad tests a different path, one that already worked.

Pick one interruption
  • A timer from the Clock app (easiest alone). Before you start, set a 2-minute timer in Clock, then go straight back to the test install. When it fires over the app, stop it from the banner.
  • A FaceTime call or phone call to the iPad from your phone, about 2 minutes in. Decline it, or answer and hang up, from the banner.
Then
  1. With the timer running (or your phone ready), send Test B out with 🚽. Note the time.
  2. Let the interruption happen and dismiss it from the banner. Planbook should never leave the screen.
  3. Don't touch the app. At 5 minutes: tint, and tone?
  4. If the 5-minute tone was silent: touch the screen once, somewhere harmless like an empty part of the header, then hands off again. At 10 minutes: tint, and tone? This tells us which half of the recovery failed.
  5. If the 5-minute tone played: don't touch anything. At 10 minutes: tint, and tone?
  6. Tap Return.

4
The failure ~15 min

Try to make it go erratic

Late on 16 August the tone played when a pass was returned, played when a new pass was sent out, and then stopped playing altogether. Nobody knows why, and nobody wrote down what had just been done. This run tries to recreate that: lots of passes, returns and switches, with every sound written down the moment it happens.

  1. Send Test A out. Wait for the 5-minute tone.
  2. While A is overdue, send Test B out. Listen.
  3. Return Test A. Listen.
  4. Send Test C out, then Return them straight away. Listen at both taps.
  5. Switch to another app for 30 seconds while B is running, then come back. If B is overdue by then, a tone on coming back is correct.
  6. Lock the iPad for 30 seconds and unlock it. Listen.
  7. Wait for B's own 5 and 10 minute thresholds, hands off.
  8. If you hear something at a moment the callout above says should be silent, note the time and what you'd just done, then stop poking it and see whether the next threshold still sounds.

What happens next

This page can't take a reading from inside the app

The app keeps a log of every interruption, recovery and tone it plays, and the iPad has no way to show it. So if runs 3 or 4 fail, this page records that it failed and what you'd just done, but not why. That's still the right first step. A failure that happens again on demand is what makes it worth building a way to show that log on the iPad, and your run 4 log tells that build which steps to repeat.

If every run passes, the next step is your call on turning the tone back on for everyone. No code needed.
