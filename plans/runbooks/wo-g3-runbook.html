Before anything else

No student names on this page

The repository is public. Use initials, or "student A / B / C", and keep the mapping in your head or on paper. That goes for every box you type into below.

Nothing here goes on the projector

The Signals screen names students beside their grades, absences and behavior entries. Stop mirroring before you open it. Presentation mode hides behavior entries; it does not hide the concern list.

Take a backup before box 6

Moving a threshold writes to the year. Put every threshold back undoes it, but a backup taken first costs a minute and means nothing on this page can lose a grade.

Where everything is

One screen, one dialog

Signals is the last pill on every class screen's switcher, and the home screen's Who needs you panel opens it too. Concern is on the left and praise on the right. The line under each name is the rule's own sentence, built from the numbers that fired it. That sentence is what box 2 checks.

The chips above the lists narrow a column to one rule, and Sorted by orders the concern list only. The praise column keeps one ranking, the biggest climb first, on purpose. 🔕 Thresholds in the screen's header opens the numbers behind every rule, which is box 6. The third panel, below the two columns, is The quiet middle, which is box 5.

A rule the term is still too short for says so under the lists, as not enough term yet. That is different from a rule nobody qualified for, and the record tables below have room for both.

Two sittings

Two weekly runs, a week apart

Why it matters. A list that reads the same every week stops being read, and the feature dies without anyone deciding to kill it. Two things should make this week's list differ from next week's: a week of new grades and attendance, and the cooldown. A student you contact about a rule drops off the list for that rule for 14 days, then comes back.

This is also WO-4.5's last open line, word for word, so closing it here closes both.

Run 1: today, before box 6
  1. Open Signals with every class showing and Sorted by on its first option.
  2. Write down how many students are in each column, and the initials of the top five in each.
  3. Send at least one real message this week from the concern list, through the send flow, so it is logged. That is the contact the cooldown should act on.
Run 2: a week later
  1. Same screen, same settings. Count both columns again and note the top five.
  2. The student you contacted should be gone from the rule you wrote about, with a muted suppressed row under the column naming the contact and the date they come back.
  3. Decide whether the two runs are visibly different, which is the whole question. Different names at the top is the strongest answer.

Real data ~45 min

Every flag, reproduced by hand

For each rule, pick one real student it fired for, read the sentence under their name, and check its numbers against the Scores and Attendance screens. The question is not whether you agree the student needs attention. It is whether the sentence's numbers are true.

Three things to know while you check:

  • Windows count meetings, not days. "4 absences in the last 20 meetings" means the class's last 20 recorded meetings, not the last 20 calendar days. A dropped day is not a meeting.
  • Attendance % is Roll Call!'s formula: (P + T + E + D) ÷ (P + T + A + E + D). An excused absence and a dismissal count as present.
  • A grade recovery produces "grade rose", not a turnaround. The turnaround only fires from attendance or behavior, the facts that carry a date.

Real data

An improving B− outranks a steady A

The praise column ranks by how much a student climbed, never by how high they are. That is the point of the whole phase: "top of the class" names the same four students every week, and "up 14 points" names someone new.

  1. In the praise column, find a student in the B− range or below who is there for Grade rose.
  2. Find a steady A student who is also in the column, most likely for A run of strong scores.
  3. Check that the climber is above the A student. A run of strong scores is ranked last among the praise rules by design, so it can never head a column that holds a climber.

Your judgement

The quiet middle names students you'd lost track of

The third panel lists students who are not flagged, not praised and not contacted this term, per class, longest-quiet first. A note to self doesn't take a student off it; it moves them down, because writing yourself a note is not being in touch with anyone.

On 27 August the panel read as "a wall this early", because almost nothing had been written down yet. Your call then was to watch it rather than change what qualifies. This box is that re-reading, four weeks in, and it also closes the testing checklist's open "quiet middle at real length" line.

  1. Filter to one class. Read the top of its quiet middle.
  2. Ask yourself honestly: are these the students you would have said you'd lost track of?
  3. Note whether the panel is still a wall or has become a short list worth reading.

Your judgement

The thresholds you actually want

Open 🔕 Thresholds on the Signals screen. Every number that decides a list is in it. Change any that disagree with how you'd judge your own classes. Leave alone any you're happy with: an unchanged threshold stays the default, so a better default in a later build still reaches you. Put every threshold back returns all of them to the defaults.

Do this after box 3's first run, and not between the two runs, or the second run measures your edit instead of the week. If you'd rather tune now, take box 3's first run again after you finish, and count the week from then.
