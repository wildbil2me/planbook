Nothing on this grid is stored twice. Seven of the eighteen items are derived — six assignment due dates and one term boundary — and none of them is in events[]. Move Cell Structure Test in the assignment editor and it moves here with no other action, which is WO-6.2's first acceptance line. The ↗ is what makes that visible rather than merely true: it says "this is edited elsewhere", and tapping it goes there.
Colour is urgency; the glyph is the kind. Twelve kinds and twelve colours would be a thing to memorise, which is the reason src/assignments.css gives for .cat-chip carrying no colour per category. So four bands do the work — not meeting · in your diary · due from you · overdue-ish — and the icon says which of the twelve it is. The legend is on the panel foot rather than behind a button, because a grammar nobody is shown is a grammar nobody has.
Sep 7 tints the whole cell; Sep 10 only tints a chip. A holiday is a fact about the day, so the cell takes src/attendance.css's own covered wash and the teacher learns it once. A planned drop is a fact about two classes, so it stays a chip with two class dots on it — one event naming two classes, never two events.
Mon–Fri, or the full seven? Five columns give each day 20% of the width instead of 14%, which on an iPad is the difference between a title and an ellipsis — and the weekend holds nothing this app models. But a Saturday field trip or an away meet has nowhere to go, and a teacher who types one and cannot find it will not trust the calendar again. Drawn at five so the cost of seven is visible. The owner's call.
The chips are not 44px, and every other control in this app is. Four items in a September cell at a 44px floor is a month that needs two screens. The proposal is that the cell is the target — tap anywhere and the day opens — and the chips stay at 12px inside it, which is the same call src/home.css's coarse block makes for .class-card-state. This needs a thumb on the real tablet before WO-6.3 commits, exactly as the score cell's input type did before WO-3.5.
Month · Week is a second segmented control. src/assignments.css already ships .screen-nav, whose aria-label is "Screens for this class" and whose module (src/screen-nav.js) resolves through src/views.js's class screens. This one switches a date range on an app-level screen. Same shape, different meaning — so either the component gets generalised (and its ARIA and its module with it) or the app carries two segmented controls that are identical to look at. Drawn as the second one, values copied literally so the two cannot drift by 2px. Cheaper to settle before WO-6.3 than after.
What happens to “Days off”? WO-2.3 shipped a dialog that authors no-school and dropped, reached from a button on the home panel and from the 📅 in a covered column head. WO-6.1's editor authors all eight kinds including those two. Two authoring surfaces for the same two kinds is the "two controls meaning one thing" defect that reopened Phase 1 (WO-1.13). Either the days-off dialog becomes a filtered view of the event editor, or it goes and its two doors re-point here. Drawn as though it has gone — note that the home panel's 📅 Days off button is 📅 Calendar on the glance page.

The rail is the bridge to the registry. Colour says which class, the ring says whether — green taken, amber not taken, hollow-grey dropped or covered. It is the only thing on the calendar that reports attendance, and it reports it for a day that isn't today, which the home screen's state line cannot do. Every dot is a way into that class's column on that date.
Five dots with no labels is a legend problem. The colours come from avatarClass(id), so they are stable and they are the same ten a teacher already reads on the class cards and in the roster — but a dot has no initials on it and there is no key on this panel. Either the rail gets initials inside the dots (11px is very small), or the week view carries its own five-class key, or the dots stay a hover/tap affordance and the title is the whole answer. Drawn as the third, which is the one that fails silently on a tablet.
At 1024px the week becomes two columns, not five. Five 20%-wide columns of wrapped titles is five columns of two words each. The month keeps its five at every width, because dropping one would put Wednesday underneath Monday, which is not a calendar.

Two delete buttons is the price of materializing, and it is worth paying. The alternative is an RRULE and a table of exceptions — the roadmap's RRULE is V2, if ever. A flat list a teacher can hand-edit in a restored backup is the whole reason the data model chose this; the cost is that "delete" is now a question with two answers, and the dialog has to ask it out loud rather than guess.
Which kinds take a class list, and which are school-wide? no-school is school-wide by definition and dropped refuses an empty list (src/calendar.js's rule, already shipped). The other six are undecided: a trip belongs to a class, a conference belongs to a student, and grades-due is arguably per-class since terms differ. studentId is in the schema and nothing has ever written it. Drawn with the class picker omitted entirely, which is the version that ducks the question.
Eight pills is a lot of dialog. The days-off picker has two and fits on one line at 390px; eight wraps to three. A <select> would fix that and is what src/shell.css's comment on .dayoff-kinds explicitly rejected for the iPad. Third option: the kind is chosen by where you started — the + Add on a cell offers the four common kinds and "more…" opens the rest.
