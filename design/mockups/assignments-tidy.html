… Tap an assignment’s name to edit it. Duplicate — the two squares on its row — copies an assignment into as many of the other classes you teach as you tick — each copy’s dates start on this one’s … A second copy in the same class is made with New.

… A category with nothing in it yet does not count as a zero — its weight is shared across the others until something is in it. Tap a column heading to sort what is under each category; tap it again to reverse, a third time for your order. While a sort is on, the arrows rest — ↺ Your order brings them back.

Five sortable columns — Assignment, Points, Assigned, Due, and Entered, which answers "what have I not finished putting in". Categories stay put; what is under each one sorts. A heading tap sorts, a second reverses, a third gives back your order. The owner, 2026-10-10.
A sort is a view and writes nothing. The document's order — the arrows' order, and the Scores grid's columns — is untouched by any heading tap. It is not remembered: leaving the screen or changing class puts the list back in your order. The owner, 2026-10-10.
Under a sort the arrows grey out and say why (their tooltip: Sorted by Due — tap Your order to move it), and ↺ Your order appears in the Actions column's heading, over the icons. It is drawn only while a sort is on, so it is never a dead control. The owner, 2026-10-10.
How a sort behaves. Names compare as a reader would ("Quiz 2" before "Quiz 10"); a blank date sorts last in both directions; ties keep your order. Unsorted headings carry a faint ↕, since an iPad has no hover to discover them. The owner, 2026-10-10.
The first tap gives the useful direction, not always ascending: Assignment A→Z, Points largest first, Assigned and Due soonest first, Entered fewest first — the question this screen is opened for. The second tap reverses; the third is your order. The owner, 2026-10-10.
A held column sorts by its own count under Entered, like any row, and keeps its held word — nothing is hidden by a sort. The owner, 2026-10-10.
A sort survives an edit. Change a due date while sorted by Due and the row moves to its new place when the editor closes — as a date that crosses a term edge already moves it today. The sort is not dropped to hide the jump. The owner, 2026-10-10.
The name button carries data-assignment-edit, the hook today's Edit button carries, so every route and harness check that opens the editor finds it unchanged. The owner, 2026-10-10.
The roster keeps its words, on purpose. Its rows say ✎ · Edit · Remove and stay that way: those rows are people, and Remove is the right word for taking a student off a class where a bin is not. A deliberate divergence, not a drift to tidy later. The owner, 2026-10-10.
Deferred, by the owner's call: collapsible categories — the list is one term long, and a collapsed group would hide the overdue tint and the part-filled bars inside it. Its own work order if it is missed. Considered for later: a view-only Due sort on the Scores grid, which would reorder its columns on screen and never in the document.
Not drawn, and unchanged: the past-due prompt and the "arrived from another term" line, which sit between the action row and the table, and the editor and copy dialogs, which open exactly as they do from today's Edit and Duplicate.
