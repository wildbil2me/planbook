DRAWINGS, NOT THE APP. Drawn 2026-08-09, before any of Phase 3's screens were built.

Gradebook mockups

Four screens, drawn against the app's real stylesheet — src/shell.css is linked, not copied, so these cannot drift from the design system without breaking loudly. The proposed styles live in proposed.css and are written to be lifted into src/scores.css, src/assignments.css and src/detail.css when the work orders land.

They exist to make one decision arguable before it is expensive: the three heavy gradebook screens are main-area views, not modals. The reasoning is in plans/gradebook-surfaces.md.

Every drawing carries annotations. The amber ones are open questions — places where the drawing had to guess and the guess is the owner's or a later work order's to make. There are seven of them and they are the most useful thing here.

  • Score entry grid · WO-3.5 · size L Students down, assignments across, with the name and the running grade frozen while the term scrolls under them. Flags in the cell, per-assignment averages in the footer, and the same screen drawn again with the weights not adding up.
  • Assignment list · WO-3.3 · size M Grouped by category with how much of each is entered, plus the modal editor that stays a modal. Shows what an empty category says about its own weight.
  • Per-student grade detail · WO-3.7 · size M The conference screen. Contributions that sum to the grade, what it would take to move, and the accommodation indicator drawn twice — once normally, once projected.
  • Class manager, re-cut · no work order Two variants of the row that started this: as it ships at six controls, and with Terms and Categories folded behind one door. Deliberately undecided.

Open these on the iPad. The touch pass in proposed.css is real — 44px cells, wider inputs, a narrower frozen name column — and the score grid is the screen most likely to be wrong under a thumb. Narrow the browser window on a laptop to see the frozen columns work.
