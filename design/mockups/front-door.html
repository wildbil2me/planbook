iPad, in Safari · as proposed: install steps first, the browser path last

Planbook

A gradebook and classroom assistant for teachers. Attendance, grades, the students who need you, and the messages that follow — all kept on your own device.

Install it on this iPad

About a minute, and nothing to download from an app store.

  1. Tap the Share button in Safari’s toolbar — the square with an arrow coming out of the top.
  2. Choose Add to Home Screen, then tap Add.
  3. Open Planbook from the new icon from then on, not from Safari.

Install it before you enter real grades. Safari can clear a website’s stored information after about a week of not being used. An installed app is exempt from that.

You can install it later from the banner inside the app.

What it does

  • Attendance at the start of every class, fast enough to mark while students are still walking in.
  • Grades by weighted category, set up separately for each class.
  • The students who need attention, in both directions — falling grades, missing work and missed class, and the ones who have turned a corner.
  • The message that follows, drafted and handed to your own email to read, change and send.
  • Accommodations where the work happens, out of sight by default, with a presentation mode that hides them all at once.

Where your students’ information goes

Nowhere but this device. Planbook has no server and no account. Google Drive sync is optional and holds only the one file Planbook made. The privacy policy and the guide for administrators say it in full.

A front door, not a gate. The steps come first and the way past comes last, so a teacher reads why before she reads how to skip it. The way past is a real 44px button, worded for the device (in Safari), and secondary: there is no primary button on this panel, because the thing to do next happens in Safari’s own toolbar, not on the page. The caution is one paragraph, beside the steps that fix it, in the palette about.html uses for the same sentence. The two lower panels are about.html’s words, shortened here only to fit the drawing; the build takes them, not a retyping.

iPad, in Safari · the alternative: no browser path at all

Planbook

A gradebook and classroom assistant for teachers. Attendance, grades, the students who need you, and the messages that follow — all kept on your own device.

On an iPad, Planbook runs from the home screen

About a minute, and nothing to download from an app store.

  1. Tap the Share button in Safari’s toolbar — the square with an arrow coming out of the top.
  2. Choose Add to Home Screen, then tap Add.
  3. Open Planbook from the new icon.

Why not in Safari? Safari can clear a website’s stored information after about a week of not being used, and with it a term of grades. An installed app is exempt from that.

…the same two panels below.

The only gate that pays for itself, and still a gate. It is the one browser where a tab genuinely loses data, so refusing it buys something real. What it costs: a teacher on a school-managed iPad where Add to Home Screen is restricted has no way in at all, and somebody who only wants a look has to install first. The caution stops being one paragraph beside the steps and becomes the reason the page exists, which leans towards the warning screen trap 3 forbids.

Laptop, in Edge or Chrome

Planbook

A gradebook and classroom assistant for teachers. Attendance, grades, the students who need you, and the messages that follow — all kept on your own device.

Use it here, or install it

Both keep everything on this computer.

Installing gives Planbook its own window and lets it open with no internet connection. Choose the install icon at the right-hand end of the address bar, or Install Planbook in the browser’s menu.

Whichever you choose, download a backup now and then from the ⤓ button in the app.

…the same two panels below.

No caution here, and the browser path is the primary. Edge and Chrome on a laptop do not clear storage the way iOS Safari does, so a teacher who stays in a tab loses nothing by it. Installing is offered as a better window, not as data safety. The install itself is the browser’s own control, in the address bar, so the page describes it rather than pretending to be it: a PWA is installed from the page it is.

Firefox on a laptop, or a Chromebook with installing turned off

Planbook

A gradebook and classroom assistant for teachers. Attendance, grades, the students who need you, and the messages that follow — all kept on your own device.

Planbook works in this browser

Everything stays on this computer.

Some browsers cannot install web apps, and some school-managed devices have installing turned off. Planbook does everything in an ordinary tab.

Download a backup now and then from the ⤓ button in the app. It is the copy that can bring a school year back.

…the same two panels below.

Can the app tell this case from the last one? Firefox can be told apart by what it lacks. A Chromebook whose admin has switched installing off probably cannot: the page sees an ordinary Chrome. Drawn as its own state because the words differ, but if the build cannot detect it, the laptop door above is what that Chromebook shows, and its install sentence describes a control that is not there. That is the implementer’s to establish, and the owner’s to accept or reword.
