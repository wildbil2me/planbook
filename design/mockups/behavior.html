Tap two — the sheet, and the entry is written by the time it closes

Owen Bennett

What kind of entry
One tap and it is written
Or say more (optional)

Entries are never edited or deleted. If you get one wrong, write another — the log is what you wrote down at the time, and that is the whole reason it is worth anything in a meeting six weeks from now.

Two taps means the quick entries write a complete record on their own. Tap the door, tap Off task, and the entry exists — the subject is the chip's own words, the kind comes from the strip above, the time is now, and the sheet closes. The two fields under them are WO-4.4's "optional detail" and are genuinely optional: a teacher who never touches them still has a log. Write it down is for when she has used them.
Behavior and note are the record's two kinds, and the strip comes first for a reason. docs/data-model.md gives the entry a kind of behavior or note, and they are not the same act — one is about conduct and one is a reminder to yourself. It is chosen before the quick entries are read because the log is append-only: a wrong kind cannot be fixed later, only annotated.
Are those six chips fixed, or the teacher's own? Drawn fixed, which needs no schema at all — they write the subject the data model already documents. But a teacher whose six most common entries are not these six will use none of them and type every time, which is the flow this work order exists to avoid. Making them editable means a per-teacher list, which means a settings block, which means docs/data-model.md and a decision about whether it travels in the year document. Cheaper to answer before WO-4.4 is cut than after.
Do the praise chips belong in the same sheet as the conduct ones? Two of the six are good news, and Phase 4's whole argument is that praise is not decoration. But a sheet opened from a control labelled write something down that leads with Off task teaches a teacher that this is where trouble goes, and she will not think of it when a student does something well. Drawn together, deliberately, with the two good ones last — which may be exactly backwards.

This is the one supports-derived thing in the whole Phase 4 room, and it wears the component WO-3.8 already shipped. Same sentence-then-scope shape, same single reveal button, same rule that the names appear only after a deliberate tap and never in presentation mode (src/assignments.css hides .accommodation-prompt outright in the print and projected paths). The drawing restyles none of it — the prompt is lifted whole, which is what CLAUDE.md means by copy rather than re-derive.
Neither half of what this prompt reads exists yet. WO-4.4's re-homed acceptance line — "marking a student absent for the Nth time surfaces an attendance-related plan clause if one exists" — needs a field on students[].supports that no schema has, and an N that no threshold block has. The work order says both are its to shape. The drawing shows the shape of the answer and cannot choose either: is the clause a free-text field beside the existing ones, or a kind of its own? Is N a signals threshold, or the same number the attendance rule already uses? Both are the owner's, and both change docs/data-model.md.

Append-only, drawn. The Sep 24 entry is wrong and it is still there: its subject is struck through, the correction rides in the body, and nothing was edited or removed. Roll Call! made hall passes append-only after matching rows by name + time proved fragile — same reasoning, same answer, and the visible consequence is a log a teacher can read in a hearing. The strikethrough is on the subject and not the body, because what was retracted is the claim.
How does a correction know which entry it corrects? The drawing shows one entry struck through and the correction inside it, which is the readable version and implies a field the record does not have — the documented shape is { id, studentId, at, kind, audience, subject, body } with nothing pointing at another entry. Two honest options: a correctsId, which is a schema change; or the correction as an ordinary later entry that simply says so, which needs no schema and cannot draw the strikethrough. Drawn the first way. The owner's call, and it is a data-model change either way it goes.

There is no log card here at all, and no line saying one was hidden. A card that redacts its own bodies still tells a room of thirty that four things have been written down about this student. So the card is not built, the way WO-1.9's acceptance puts it: the data is absent from the DOM rather than hidden in it.
And the decision is not made on this screen. src/supports.js is the one place that answers whether sensitive content is on screen, and the card asks it rather than testing presentation mode itself — the same posture proposed-phase6.css takes with a review date, where the calendar view holds no presentationMode() test because the model hands it an empty list. Two askers is two answers eventually, and wo-sweep.mjs counts the askers.
Is a note to self as sensitive as a behavior entry? Both are suppressed together here. "Said he is working nights" is arguably worse on a wall than "off task" — it is the student's own disclosure — so suppressing both is the safe reading. But it also means a teacher cannot see her own reminders on a projected screen at all, and the reminders are the half that has nothing to do with conduct. Drawn suppressed together.
