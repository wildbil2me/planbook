Three columns, and the order is the order of the work. Pick the template, write it, watch it resolve. The preview is not behind a button: WO-5.2 asks for it so that "a broken field is caught at authoring time rather than at send time", and a preview a teacher has to open is a preview she stops opening by the third template.
The body is a plain textarea and the tokens in it are not highlighted. Colouring them here would have been one line of CSS and a contenteditable in the build — this app has none, and the first one arrives with its own selection, paste and undo problems on an iPad. The preview is where a token stops being text, which is also where a teacher can see whether it was the right one.
The palette says the rule out loud rather than documenting it by omission. WO-5.2's Deliverables call the palette "documentation of the refusal list, by omission", and omission alone is a weak way to document a rule nobody has been told exists. The amber block is not a warning to the teacher — she is not being cautioned against doing something, she is being told the door does not exist. CLAUDE.md § Accommodations is the standard this control has to meet.
Where is this screen reached from? It is drawn with All classes selected, because a template is not about one class — {{class.name}} resolves per draft, at send time, from the class the signal row came out of. But that leaves the arrival unanswered: the class switcher is wrong for the same reason, Your details holds the teacher's name and email and is the nearest existing home, and a sixth screen-nav segment is the thing WO-6.6 already ruled against twice. This is the owner's call and it is an Open line on WO-5.2, not something a drawing settles.
Should the starter templates ship filled in, or empty? Five are drawn in the list and WO-5.2's Deliverables ask for "starter templates for both tones and each audience". Written well they are the fastest way to teach what a merge field is; written once they are also the sentences a hundred guardians will read in the same words. The drawing takes no position on how many, or whether the first launch should open on one of them already loaded.

This is the state the whole room was drawn for. A template that reads perfectly against the first student on the roster is blocked for the fourth, and nothing about the template is wrong. So the student is a control rather than a caption, and the block strip names the student in every line — a message that says only "guardian.name did not resolve" sends its reader looking in the template for a fault that is not there.
{{missing.count}} resolved to 0 and {{missing.list}} did not resolve at all, in the same sentence, and that is src/merge-fields.js's ruling rather than this drawing's: a count is a value at zero and a list of nothing is not. It draws badly on purpose here — "0 assignments marked missing: {{missing.list}}" is what that template says about a student with none, which is the template being wrong for this row and exactly what a preview is for.

This draft cannot be sent · 2 fields did not resolve
{{studnet.first}} Not a merge field. Check the spelling against the list.
{{student.supports.accommodations}} Accommodation, medical and plan details can never be merged into a message.
Three ways to fail, one treatment in the body, and that is a ruling rather than a shortcut. src/merge-fields.js tells refused, unknown and unresolved apart by a named code and never by the shape of the output — all three hand the teacher's own token back intact. Drawing three different in-body treatments would put the distinction where the message is, which is the one place it must not be: a body that renders a refusal differently from a typo reads as though the refusal were a kind of value. The strip is the layer that is allowed to explain.
The refused line says what the rule is, not that something went wrong. The teacher typed that field on purpose; the honest answer is that the app will not carry it, in one sentence, with the token still visible so she can delete it herself. Nothing is deleted for her and nothing is silently blanked — a body that came back reading clean would be a body she could send.
Does the strip stay when nothing is wrong? It is drawn that way — green, saying "nothing blocked · 8 fields resolved" — on the argument that a strip appearing only on failure is read as an error banner, while one that is always there is a report, and WO-5.3 reads the same blocked flag to decide whether its own send button is live. The cost is a permanent block of chrome on a narrow screen. Owner's call, and an Open line on WO-5.2.
At 390px — the stacking order, which is a decision and not a fallback
The strip below is 390px wide, the narrowest phone the suite supports. One column, and the order is list · editor · preview · palette: the palette is a reference a teacher consults, the preview is the thing she is watching while she types, so the preview goes above it rather than below. Drawn as a strip rather than argued in prose because the order is the whole decision.

The palette at the foot of a phone screen is the weakest thing in this drawing. "Tap a chip to insert at the cursor" is a desk gesture; on a phone the keyboard is up, the palette is below the fold, and tapping it may well close the field it is meant to type into. Nothing here is measured — no thumb has touched it. It wants a reading on the real tablet before WO-5.2 is dispatched, and the alternative worth having in hand is a palette that opens as a sheet over the keyboard rather than a column under it.
