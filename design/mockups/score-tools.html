What this drawing is for

The owner's list of 2026-10-01, for the score grid that ships today: filter by assignment type, filter by student, keep the assignment names on screen, a cleaner sideways scroll on the laptop, and a focused cell that is always visible. The last three are one change: the grid gets a box of its own that scrolls both ways (proposed-scores.css § SCORE SCROLL BOX). The first two are a toolbar above it, the attendance screen's own (§ SCORE TOOLBAR). English III, Period 2, fourteen students, twelve assignments; every name and score is invented.

The overall grade stays where it is, and a category's average is a second column beside it while a category is picked. Filtering by student is type-to-narrow, not a jump to one student's page. Both the owner, 2026-10-01, in conversation before this was drawn.

Scroll it, both ways. The assignment row stays on top, the name and grade stay on the left, and the top-left corner stays put in both directions. The horizontal scrollbar is at the bottom of the box, so it's always on screen. A trackpad swipe settles with a column's left edge against the frozen edge (scroll-snap-type: x proximity), never halfway under it. Now repeat the Shift+Tab test from the first frame: each cell scrolls clear of the frozen columns as the caret reaches it, and Tab and Enter going down keep the caret clear of the stuck header. Nothing in src/scores.js changes for any of this.

The toolbar is .attendance-toolbar value for value: .search-box first, then .pills, all worn as shipped. A teacher who filters attendance already knows how to filter this. Neither filter is remembered: both reset whenever the screen is opened, for the reason the calendar's class filter gives. A grid that quietly opens on Quizzes because of last Tuesday is a grid missing two-thirds of its columns with no explanation. The printed grade sheet ignores both filters.

Open 1 · how tall is the box? Answered: the box is as tall as the screen, and what sits above it scrolls away. (Owner, 2026-10-01.) Drawn at a fixed 420px to fit this page. In the app it would be calc(100dvh − what sits above it), so the whole box, header and scrollbar included, fits on one screen once the page is scrolled to it. The summary, flag bar and toolbar above it scroll out of the way as a page normally does. The alternative is to pin those too and give the grid whatever is left. That keeps the flag buttons on screen, but on an iPad in landscape it leaves very few rows.

Open 2 · does a swipe that reaches the grid's edge carry on into the page? Answered: no, it stops at the edge. (Owner, 2026-10-01, ruled from the desk. The iPad reading is still owed as a 👤 line.) Drawn with overscroll-behavior: contain: it doesn't. A finger in the grid scrolls only the grid. Whether that feels right or feels stuck on the iPad is a question for your thumb, not for the desk.

Open 3 · the head is tall when it's stuck. Answered: keep all four lines. (Owner, 2026-10-01.) Four lines per column: name, points, category, due date. When stuck, that's about 84px of every scroll position. Drawn as is. The alternative is a compact head that drops the due line, at the cost of two sets of styles for one head.

Five quiz columns, and a third frozen column: each student's Quizzes average. The overall grade has not moved and still means the same thing. The new column names its category in its own header every time and is tinted, so at a glance it can't be mistaken for a second overall grade. It shows a percentage and no letter, the same rule as the class average. The figure comes from categoryPercentage() in src/grade-engine.js, which already exists, so the screen does no new arithmetic. Quiz columns that aren't shown are not drawn at all, rather than hidden. Tab and → stop at the last shown column, because a hidden cell that can still take the caret is a caret nobody can see.

Open 4 · one category at a time, or several? Answered: one, because a single category's average is what gets checked against the SIS. (Owner, 2026-10-01.) Drawn as one plus All, like the attendance and signals pills. Picking two (Quizzes and Homework) is possible, but then the third column has no single name and needs either two columns or none.

Open 5 · does the summary line gain the category's class average? Answered: yes. (Owner, 2026-10-01.) Drawn: Quizzes average appears beside the class average while Quizzes is picked. Leaving it out keeps the line from growing, at the cost of a number you might reasonably want.

Open 6 · should the pills show weights? Answered: names only. (Owner, 2026-10-01.) Drawn with names only. Each column's chip already shows the weight, and Quizzes 20% on a filter button reads like a setting.

Open 7 · what does a name match on? Answered: the start of a first, last or preferred name, and Escape clears the search box. (Owner, 2026-10-01.) Drawn: the start of the first or last name, ignoring capitals. Should a preferred name the roster holds also match? And should Escape in the search box clear it? The grid deliberately does nothing on Escape, but the search box isn't part of the grid. And the attendance search changes to match. When this was drawn it matched letters anywhere in a name and ignored the preferred name, so two identical-looking boxes would have answered “ma” differently. The owner ruled the same day that both use this rule (start of a first, last or preferred name), and the build changes src/attendance.js in the same work order.
