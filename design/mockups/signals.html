What to do
Every number under a sentence is the number the rule measured. WO-4.1 hands a rule its own measured values and nothing else — not the document, not the clock, not the threshold it just crossed — so that an explanation can never drift from the arithmetic behind it. This card is that contract drawn: the sentence on top, the values under it, and the threshold named as yours with a way to change it, because an absent threshold key IS its default and a teacher who cannot see that has been given an opinion she cannot argue with.
The third rule is drawn as context, not as a flag. Two scores under 60% against a threshold of three: showing it is the difference between "why is he here?" and "here is everything I know". Whether a card may show a rule that did not fire is a question below — it is a short step from there to a card that argues for a signal the engine declined to raise.
One disabled control is the whole of Phase 5 on this drawing. Draft an email is the door WO-5.3 and WO-5.4 both reach for by name — "entry points from the signal card", "contact history on the signal card" — and drawing the draft, the template picker or the audience list here would be pre-empting decisions that belong to that phase. What the door buys now is that the card's shape does not have to change when outreach lands.

A third list, not a third column. The two columns above share a ranking — how much changed — and this one does not: it is ranked by how long it has been, which is a different question with a different unit. Putting it beside them would imply it shares their order.
Is the quiet middle a panel or a page? Drawn twice on purpose: the glance page reaches it through a The quiet middle · 9 control in a panel header, and here it is a panel of its own under the two columns. If it is a panel here, the control up there needs somewhere to land; if it is a page, this screen has two states and the switcher has nothing to say about which one you are on. Cheaper to answer before WO-4.5 is cut than after.
Presentation mode — the screen closes rather than redacts

Not while you are projecting.

This screen is a list of named students who are struggling, which is the one thing in Planbook that should never be on a classroom wall. Presentation mode is on — turn it off with the screen button in the header and this comes straight back.

Closed, or redacted to initials? Every other surface in the app redacts: the roster hides plan details behind a tap, the calendar drops review dates out of the list it is handed, the student detail shows the grade and hides supports. This one is drawn as the first screen that refuses altogether, on the grounds that redacting a ranked list of who is failing to "OB, SH, RE" protects nobody in a room of thirty who know each other's initials. The cost is real: a teacher who leaves presentation mode on all period cannot use the screen at all. The owner's call, and the most consequential question on this page.
The fifth segment at 390px — the measurement, not the argument
Both strips below are 390px wide, which is the narrowest phone the suite supports. The top one is .screen-nav exactly as it ships — overflow-x: auto, so the fifth segment is reachable only by swiping a control a teacher does not know scrolls. That is WO-6.6's own trap, one segment further along. The bottom one is the same strip with flex-wrap: wrap, drawn with an inline style because this sheet may not restyle a class src/assignments.css owns.
Five segments, wrapped — or four and a door somewhere else? The wrap is one declaration in a shipped stylesheet and it makes every segment visible and thumb-sized at 390px, at the cost of a two-row strip on a phone. The alternatives, both real: shorten the labels (Roll · Work · Scores · Month · Signals), which trades a scroll for five words a teacher has to learn; or keep four and reach this screen from the glance page only, which is the cul-de-sac WO-6.6 was opened to remove. Drawn wrapped. The owner's call, and it is a change to a shipped sheet, so it belongs in the work order rather than in this drawing.
What makes one concern worse than another? WO-4.2 says the list is "ordered by severity" and nothing anywhere defines severity across rules. Is a 12-point fall worse than five absences? Than a 61% that has been 61% all term? The drawing invents an order — real deltas first, biggest first, then countable rules — and that order is a guess. It is the one open question here that changes what the engine has to return rather than only what the screen draws.
May the list be re-sorted? The toolbar states the ranking instead of offering it, on the grounds that a "sort by grade" control puts the level back in charge of the order this phase exists to replace. But a teacher looking for the four students she must talk to before Friday may reasonably want the failing ones together. Drawn as a statement, not a control.
