What to do
Every number under a sentence is the number the rule measured. WO-4.1 hands a rule its own measured values and nothing else — not the document, not the clock, not the threshold it just crossed — so that an explanation can never drift from the arithmetic behind it. This card is that contract drawn: the sentence on top, the values under it, and the threshold named as yours with a way to change it, because an absent threshold key IS its default and a teacher who cannot see that has been given an opinion she cannot argue with.
The third rule is drawn as context, not as a flag. Two scores under 60% against a threshold of three: showing it is the difference between "why is he here?" and "here is everything I know". Whether a card may show a rule that did not fire is a question below — it is a short step from there to a card that argues for a signal the engine declined to raise.
One disabled control is the whole of Phase 5 on this drawing. Draft an email is the door WO-5.3 and WO-5.4 both reach for by name — "entry points from the signal card", "contact history on the signal card" — and drawing the draft, the template picker or the audience list here would be pre-empting decisions that belong to that phase. What the door buys now is that the card's shape does not have to change when outreach lands.

This screen is a list of named students who are struggling, which is the one thing in Planbook that should never be on a classroom wall. Presentation mode is on — turn it off with the screen button in the header and this comes straight back.

Closed — the owner's ruling, 2026-08-20, and it is the first screen in the app that refuses rather than redacts. Initials protect nobody in a room of thirty who know each other's initials, and this is the one surface whose entire content is a ranked list of named students in trouble. So it does not draw itself at all.

The cost is accepted rather than argued away: a teacher who leaves presentation mode on for a whole period cannot use this screen for that period. That is why the refusal names the control that undoes it in the same sentence — the way out is one tap in the header, and the screen says so rather than looking broken.

Attendance outranks grade drops — the owner's ruling, 2026-08-20. This was the one question in the room that changes what the engine returns rather than what a screen draws, and it is settled: a student who is not in the room is the more urgent problem than a student whose grade slipped, because the second is often a symptom of the first and because attendance is the one a teacher cannot fix later. So the concern column bands — attendance rules first, everything else after — and within each band the biggest change leads, which is the rule the whole phase is built on. Ruth's five absences now sit above Owen's twelve-point fall for that reason, and the column head says so out loud.

What is deliberately not settled: where missing work, low scores and tardies sit relative to a grade fall. They keep the drawn order — real deltas first, then counts — and WO-4.2 may reorder them without re-opening this ruling. The ruling is attendance leads; the rest is a default until someone has a reason.
