D · Keys at 640px

Marking from the keyboard

One key per student, and your hand never leaves the keyboard. Press ↓ to land on the first name, then type a letter for each student as you look up at the room — the selection moves down the list on its own, so a class of thirty is thirty keystrokes.

↓↑
Next student, or back to the one above. The first ↓ picks up the top name.
P
Present.
T
Tardy. The time is recorded with it.
A
Absent.
E
Event — an excused absence.
D
Dismissed. The time is recorded, and an open hall pass is closed with it.
Esc
Stop. Nothing is selected, and the letters do nothing until you press an arrow again.
?
This list.

A letter sets the mark rather than stepping through the cycle, so an absence is one key whatever the cell was reading before it. Everything saves as you type it, the same as a tap — there is nothing to submit.

The keys go quiet wherever a tap would be refused: while you are typing in the search box, while a dialog is open over the grid, on a past day that has not had its ✏ pressed, and on a day the class didn’t meet. And they only ever write on the day the screen says it is editing, which is today unless you have unlocked something else.

640, not 900. This is prose and a key list, and a line of prose much past 80 characters is harder to read, not easier. At 640 the dialog is about a third shorter than at 480. The number is the drawing’s guess. Not ruled on.
