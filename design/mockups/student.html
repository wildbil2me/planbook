The name in the switcher is a breadcrumb, not a tab. (Owner, 2026-08-09.) This screen was drawn as a fourth peer tab labelled A student on the other two mockups, and it is not one — you cannot enter it without first choosing a student. There are three tabs now, and Owen Bennett appears in the strip only because a name was tapped to get here; switching to Assignments or Scores takes it away. So WO-3.7 owns no navigation target of its own — it owns the entry points on the screens that name students, and the way back.
The contributions sum to the grade, and that is the point of the table. 31.20 + 16.25 + 8.00 + 11.00 = 66.45. WO-3.7's first acceptance line asks for exactly this, and a guardian who can follow the arithmetic on the screen is a guardian who is not arguing about whether the number is real.
The empty category is shown, not hidden. Participation holds nothing, so its weight redistributes across the other four rather than counting as a zero — the row says so in the caution wash. Hiding it is how a teacher concludes the app lost an assignment.
"What it would take to move" gives two figures and a third route. One number is a target; two numbers are a conversation, and the missing-homework line is the one a fifteen-year-old can act on tonight. The arithmetic is reproducible by hand — WO-3.7's third acceptance line — and the 75% figure was solved for, not estimated.
Extra credit is real, so the "to move" figure has to count it. Owner, 2026-08-09: a zero-point assignment scored 5 is +5 earned points in its category. That makes the bonus a genuine route to the next band — often the cheapest one, since it adds to the numerator without adding to the denominator — and a "what it would take" figure that ignored it would be wrong in the student's favour, which is the worst direction to be wrong in front of a parent. The figures on this page do not yet include it; they were solved before the decision. WO-3.7 must, and its acceptance line already says the figure has to be reproducible by hand.
Which raises a question this screen should answer out loud. If a bonus is outstanding, "scoring 75% on everything left" is ambiguous — 75% of a 0-point assignment is 0 points. The remaining-work list and the arithmetic behind it need to treat bonus work as a separate line rather than folding it into "everything left". Not yet drawn.
The accommodation indicator, and the same screen projected
This is the most sensitive control in the gradebook, so it is drawn twice. Left: normal use — a neutral dot and a count, no word, no icon that names a plan, no colour that reads as an alert. It says "there is something here to open", which is what a teacher needs to know she has an obligation, and nothing a parent reading upside down or a room reading off a wall can decode. Right: presentation mode, where it is gone entirely — not greyed, not collapsed. src/supports.js answers that question for the whole app and this element obeys it rather than having a rule of its own.

Open question — should this screen be reachable in presentation mode at all? WO-3.7's acceptance line only requires that no supports data appears.
