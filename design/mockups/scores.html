The two frozen columns are the whole idea. Narrow the window until the grid scrolls sideways: the student and their grade stay put while the assignments run under them. A real Quarter 1 has twenty-odd columns, so without this a teacher four columns in is typing a number into an anonymous row. The grade is frozen beside the name rather than parked at the far right, because it is the number she is watching change while she types.
Flags are said twice — in the fill and in a corner glyph — for the reason src/attendance.css gives about the registry: a state carried only by a background disappears on hover, in print, and from the back of a room. The palettes are the registry's own, so late here is the same orange as tardy there. Blank is styled as nothing at all, deliberately: blank means ungraded and affects no grade, and a styled blank looks like a state somebody chose.
The grade column is not coloured by band. No red for a failing number, no green for a passing one. This app colours nothing else by judgement, it would be the loudest thing on a screen that gets projected, and it would put a red row beside a student's name in front of the class. The letter carries the meaning.
The per-assignment average in the footer is the cheapest useful thing on the screen: reading down and finding the class averaged 61% on the Osmosis Quiz is a fact most gradebooks make you go and compute. Not in WO-3.5's deliverables — an addition to argue about.
About the numbers. The scores are invented and most of the grades in the frozen column are illustrative rather than computed — with one exception that matters: Owen Bennett's 66.5% is real arithmetic on the row above it, and the student detail drawing breaks it down to contributions that sum back to it. When a drawing shows a number and its workings on two screens, the two have to agree, or the thing being reviewed is a rounding error instead of a design.
Open question — the cell is type="text" with inputmode="decimal", not type="number". WO-3.1's weight field is a real number input and the iPad sitting passed it, but that field is 58px in a dialog with one of it on screen. Here there are 240 of them: the spinner eats width in a 96px column, and iOS draws its own affordances on number inputs. This needs a device before WO-3.5 commits.
Extra credit is a zero-point assignment, and needs no feature at all. Owner, 2026-08-09. The last column is Bonus: Diffusion Poster, out of 0, filed under Quizzes like any other quiz — Claire's 5 adds 5 to the Quizzes numerator and nothing to its denominator, taking her 20/40 of quizzes to 25/40 and her overall grade to 101.2%. Nothing caps it. This drawing previously showed a 0%-weight category, which was the wrong answer to the same question: a 0%-weight category contributes nothing, so the column would have been decorative.
The feature is the absence of a special case, which is why it works: no extra credit flag, no field, no category type. It falls out of a category being sum(earned) / sum(possible) rather than an average of per-assignment percentages — so this decision also pins that, and docs/data-model.md now says so.
The one thing this breaks, and it needs a hand-computed case. A category holding only zero-point assignments has possible = 0 — n/0, which is not 100% and not 0% and will crash a naive engine. A teacher reaches it by making an "Extra credit" category and putting only extra credit in it, which is the obvious thing to do. It should behave as an empty category and redistribute. Added to WO-3.4's acceptance list.

There is no grade, rather than a provisional one. The owner settled this on 2026-08-09, and it replaces what this drawing showed a few hours earlier — an amber percentage with a dotted underline. A weighted average over weights that don't add up is arithmetic nobody asked for wearing the costume of an answer, and a teacher mid-setup would have had no way to tell how far off it was. Refusing to print one is simpler and truer, and it deletes the question of what to divide by instead of answering it. Recorded in docs/data-model.md § Grade math.
The em dash is quiet, and the banner is loud. One sentence at the top explains the whole column; repeating that alarm down twenty-four rows would read as twenty-four problems with twenty-four students, when the problem is with the class, once. But the cell is not left empty — an empty cell in a grade column is indistinguishable from a rendering fault, which is the same reason the registry never draws an empty attendance cell.
Note what is not disabled. Every score field is still live and the keyboard path still works. "No grade yet" is a statement about the grade, not a lock on the screen — WO-3.1's rule was "don't block them, tell them", and that survives the change intact.
Still open — what does the class average line say here? The summary above the grid reads "Class average 84.2%". With no grades there is no average either, so it presumably becomes the same em dash — but a per-assignment footer average is a different figure, computed from points and not from weights at all, and there is no reason for those to disappear. Drawn with the footer intact and the class average absent; worth confirming.

⚠ Weights total 95%, not 100% — 5% short. Nothing is blocked: keep setting up. Any grade in this class is provisional until they add up.

Weights are percentages of the final grade and want to add up to 100. They do not have to right now — nothing is blocked while they are wrong, and Planbook will say what they come to until they add up. A category with no work in it yet doesn’t drag the grade down: its weight spreads across the categories that do have work, so an empty one costs nothing.

To stop a category counting without losing anything, set its weight to 0 — the assignments stay and the grade stops using them. Removing is the one that takes the work with it, which is why it counts what goes first.

The arithmetic lines up with the grid above. 40 + 25 + 20 + 10 + 0 = 95, which is the number the banner on the provisional grid prints — and Classwork at 10 rather than 15 is the single edit that put the class there. Participation at 0% is the state WO-3.1 offers instead of deleting, which is why a 0 is never silently removed.
This is also the crowded row, one level down. Five controls per category — a name field, a weight field, two arrows and Remove — inside a 480px dialog. It is the same pressure the class manager's row is under, and it is worth looking at on the tablet next to the class-manager drawing rather than separately.
