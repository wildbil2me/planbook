① Landing · every device, shown only to a visitor who is not installed and has nothing stored

Planbook

A gradebook and classroom assistant for teachers. Attendance, grades, the students who need you, and the messages that follow — all kept on your own device.

What it does

  • Attendance in seconds, while students are still walking in.
  • Grades by weighted category, set up for each class.
  • Who needs you — the students slipping, and the ones turning a corner.
  • The note home, drafted for you and sent from your own email.

Your students’ information stays on your device. No account, and no server of ours ever sees it. Privacy policy · Guide for administrators

One button, and no install steps. The band and the two panels are the whole page — about a screen on an iPad held upright. The pitch is four lines where about.html has five paragraph-bullets, and the privacy promise is one sentence where it has three bullets. The install steps, the iPad caution, the Firefox paragraph and the backup line are gone from this page; the iPad gets them on the next screen, and everyone else meets install as the banner’s one line later on.

Same words in about.html, per the ruling. Its What it does and Where your students’ information goes sections are cut to what is drawn here, so the door keeps lifting them and nothing is retyped. The pointers into privacy.html and docs/FERPA.md still carry the full argument.

Ask 1 — does about.html keep its Installing it section? It is the public page Google reviews and the page a principal is sent, and the door would no longer lift it. Proposed: keep it there, shortened to the two device step lists, because a reader who is not in the app still wants to know how. The door simply stops showing it.

Ask 2 — the button’s word. Get started is drawn. Open Planbook is about.html’s own word for the same white button, and using it here would make the two pages one sentence apart rather than two.

② Install · iPad or iPhone in Safari only — after Get started

Planbook

One step before you start.

Add Planbook to your home screen

Safari can clear a website’s data after a week unused. The home-screen app keeps it.

  1. Tap Share in Safari’s toolbar — the square with the arrow.
  2. Choose Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Open Planbook from the new icon.

WO-8.16’s ruling 4 stands: a door, not a gate. The steps come first and the way past is last and secondary. What changes is the amount: one sentence of why in the panel’s subtitle instead of an amber caution, and no What it does under it — the visitor has just read that. A laptop, a Chromebook and Firefox never see this screen; Get started takes them straight to ③.

Why install comes before the question, on this one device. Safari and the home-screen app keep separate storage. A teacher who sets up in Safari and installs afterwards opens the icon to an empty app with her year left behind in Safari. Installing first means no year is ever made in the wrong place.

Ask 3 — does the installed app’s first launch skip ① and ②? It does today and the drawing keeps it: an installed launch never sees the door, so opening the new icon goes straight to ③. Drawn that way because the visitor read the pitch a minute ago in Safari.
