⚠ Planbook is not installed on this device.

iPad Safari erases the stored data of any site you have not opened in about a week. Everything Planbook keeps — your roster, your attendance, your grades — is in that storage, so one quiet week over a holiday can take a term of work with it. Apps added to the home screen are exempt from that, which is the whole reason to add it.

On an iPad: tap the Share button in Safari's toolbar — the square with an arrow coming out of the top — then choose Add to Home Screen and tap Add. Open Planbook from that icon from now on.
On a computer: use the install button at the right-hand end of the address bar, or the browser menu → Install Planbook.

About Planbook

Planbook is a gradebook and communication assistant. It tracks grades and attendance, surfaces the students who need attention in both directions — concern and praise — and drafts the outreach that follows.

Everything stays on this device. There is no account and no server: your data lives in this browser's own storage, and Google Drive sync is an opt-in extra you can ignore entirely.

This build

Classes and their terms are the only things you can enter so far. The frame and the storage behind it are real — one document per school year, kept in this browser — and so is the class bar at the top. There is no roster and no gradebook yet; those come next. The backup file that gets everything back out of the browser is already here, under the ⤓ button in the header, because nothing that stores a student's name ships before the way to get it back.

Terms

Call it whatever you call it out loud — “Period 3 — Biology”, “AP Bio”, “3rd hour”. Four quarters unless you choose otherwise, and every term can be renamed, dated, added to, or removed afterwards: a new class arrives usable rather than correct. Terms belong to the class, so one class on semesters and another on quarters is an ordinary thing here.

A class is archived rather than deleted: archiving keeps everything in it and only takes it off the class bar. Deleting is offered on an archived class, once it is out of your way.

Archived

An archived class keeps everything in it — attendance, assignments, grades — and only leaves the class bar. Restoring it puts it back where it was. Deleting is the one that destroys what is in it, which is why it is offered here rather than up there.

What goes with it

Backup & restore

Planbook keeps each school year in this browser's own storage. That storage can be erased — by iPadOS after a week away, by a browser reset, by a laptop that stops starting — so a file you download and keep somewhere safe is the real safety net. Download one at the end of every week.

Download a backup

What is in this file. Everything Planbook holds for that school year: your classes, your roster, guardian and counselor contacts, attendance, grades, and your record of who you have contacted. It also contains the support details you keep on students — accommodations, IEP and 504 plans, medical needs, and behavior plans. That is on purpose: a backup that left them out would not bring your gradebook back.

It is plain text, so anyone who opens the file can read all of it. Keep it the way you keep a paper folder of the same information — somewhere only you can reach, and not in an email.

Restore from a backup

Restoring replaces one whole school year with the copy in the file — the year named inside the file, not whichever year you happen to have open. Planbook shows you what is being replaced and asks before it changes anything.
