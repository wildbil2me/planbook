About Planbook

Planbook is a gradebook and communication assistant. It tracks grades and attendance, surfaces the students who need attention in both directions — concern and praise — and drafts the outreach that follows.

Everything stays on this device. There is no account and no server: your data lives in this browser's own storage, and Google Drive sync is an opt-in extra you can ignore entirely.

This build

Attendance, your roster, and how each class is graded are what you can enter so far. The frame and the storage behind it are real — one document per school year, kept in this browser — and so are the class bar at the top and the students behind it. Marking a class is finished and in daily use, hall passes and the days a class didn't meet included.

The gradebook itself is half here. You can set up what a class is graded on and what each part is worth, but there are no assignments, no scores and no grades behind those categories yet — so nothing on this screen works out a percentage for anybody. That is what comes next. Signals, outreach and Drive sync come after it.

The backup file that gets everything back out of the browser is already here, under the ⤓ button in the header, because nothing that stores a student's name ships before the way to get it back.

Terms

Call it whatever you call it out loud — “Period 3 — Biology”, “AP Bio”, “3rd hour”. Four quarters unless you choose otherwise, and every term can be renamed, dated, added to, or removed afterwards: a new class arrives usable rather than correct. Terms belong to the class, so one class on semesters and another on quarters is an ordinary thing here. It also arrives with four grading categories adding up to 100% — tests, quizzes, homework, classwork — which are a starting point and not a guess you have to live with.

A class is archived rather than deleted: archiving keeps everything in it and only takes it off the class bar. Deleting is offered on an archived class, once it is out of your way.

Categories & weights

What is graded on, and how much each part counts. Every class has its own set, so one class weighted 40/35/25 and another weighted evenly is an ordinary thing here — Planbook never makes them match.

Categories in this class

Weights are percentages of the final grade and want to add up to 100. They do not have to right now — nothing is blocked while they are wrong, and Planbook will say what they come to until they add up. A category with no work in it yet doesn’t drag the grade down: its weight spreads across the categories that do have work, so an empty one costs nothing.

To stop a category counting without losing anything, set its weight to 0 — the assignments stay and the grade stops using them. Removing is the one that takes the work with it, which is why it counts what goes first.

Outreach

Backup & restore

Planbook keeps each school year in this browser's own storage. That storage can be erased — by iPadOS after a week away, by a browser reset, by a laptop that stops starting — so a file you download and keep somewhere safe is the real safety net. Download one at the end of every week.

Download a backup

What is in this file. Everything Planbook holds for that school year: your classes, your roster, guardian and counselor contacts, attendance, grades, and your record of who you have contacted. It also contains the support details you keep on students — accommodations, IEP and 504 plans, case managers and review dates, medical needs, and behavior plans. That is on purpose: a backup that left them out would not bring your gradebook back.

It is plain text, so anyone who opens the file can read all of it. Keep it the way you keep a paper folder of the same information — somewhere only you can reach, and not in an email.

Restore from a backup

Restoring replaces one whole school year with the copy in the file — the year named inside the file, not whichever year you happen to have open. Planbook shows you what is being replaced and asks before it changes anything.

A letter sets the mark rather than stepping through the cycle, so an absence is one key whatever the cell was reading before it. Everything saves as you type it, the same as a tap — there is nothing to submit.

The keys go quiet wherever a tap would be refused: while you are typing in the search box, while a panel is open, on a past day that has not had its ✏ pressed, and on a day the class didn’t meet. And they only ever write on the day the screen says it is editing, which is today unless you have unlocked something else.
